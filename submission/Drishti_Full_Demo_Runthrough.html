Part 1 — The Prototype Brief slides

01

Slide 1 — Introduce Drishti

Goal: give the evaluator one clear sentence about the product.

ShowSlide 1 of Drishti_Prototype_Brief.html
Stay on it for 8–10 seconds.
Speak

“Good morning. We are RENEGADES, and this is Drishti — a Catalyst-powered intelligence platform for investigation and command. It connects fragmented FIR information into an explainable workspace that helps officers review context before they act.”

02

Slide 2 — Explain the problem

Goal: make the pain real without overexplaining the dataset.

ShowSlide 2 — “Critical information exists”
Point at the three columns only if useful.
Speak

“The problem is not that information is unavailable. FIRs, people, evidence and case-progress details can sit in different records. That means an investigator or supervisor spends time manually connecting facts before they can understand what requires attention.”

03

Slide 3 — Present the solution

Goal: introduce the three-part value: discover, validate, act.

ShowSlide 3 — “One workspace”
Speak

“Drishti brings that context into one workspace. Officers can discover related FIRs and entities, validate links and evidence, and act with a clear view of case progress and district priorities. Every result is decision support, so human validation remains central.”

04

Slide 4 — Establish Catalyst credibility

Goal: explicitly show the required Catalyst deployment architecture.

ShowSlide 4 — Technology stack
Speak

“The working prototype uses a responsive web workspace, a FastAPI service on Zoho Catalyst AppSail, and a connected relational FIR subset in Catalyst Data Store through ZCQL. The browser does not directly access the database; application logic sits in the service layer.”

Say “working prototype” and “connected Data Store” confidently. Do not say that it is a full production deployment.
05

Slide 5 — Explain real-world use

Goal: make the workflow feel operational and responsible.

ShowSlide 5 — Ask → Connect → Validate → Act
Speak

“The intended workflow is simple. An officer asks a question, Drishti connects the relevant records, the officer validates evidence and confidence signals, and then a supervisor can use that context for accountable action. This supports DGP-level command, SCRB analysis and station-level investigation.”

06

Slide 6 — Transition to demo

Goal: close the deck and move immediately to proof.

ShowSlide 6 — closing statement
Then switch to the live app.
Speak

“Now I’ll show how this works in the live Catalyst deployment: from an investigative question, to linked records, evidence review, lifecycle progress and a district-ready patrol plan.”

Part 2 — Live application demo

07

Screen 1 — FIR Search

Goal: demonstrate the primary investigation entry point.

Navigate & clickFIR Search → select Operation Night Watch, or enter: Show repeat burglary accused in Bengaluru at night → click Analyse query.
Speak while results appear

“An officer can search across FIR number, person, phone, vehicle, offence, location clue or the complaint narrative. Drishti returns reviewable FIR leads and repeat profiles with the context behind them — not an automatic conclusion.”

Do not click every result. Pick one repeat-accused profile and move on.
08

Screen 2 — Intelligence Profiles

Goal: show cross-record context in one view.

Navigate & clickOpen Intelligence Profiles → use the repeat-offender result or the Repeat-offender check scenario.
Speak

“Instead of making the investigator hunt across records, Drishti gathers linked FIR history, identifiers and district context into one profile. The profile is a starting point for validation, not a finding of guilt.”

09

Screen 3 — Link Analysis

Goal: prove explainability rather than just a graph.

Navigate & clickOpen Link Analysis → run the shared phone or vehicle scenario → point to a visible reason for a link.
Speak

“Links are not decorative lines. Drishti explains the record-based reason: for example a shared phone, vehicle, narrative clue or co-accused connection. This lets the officer inspect the support for a lead before taking any next step.”

If the graph is slow, skip directly to Incident Reconstruction; do not wait on camera.
10

Screen 4 — Incident Reconstruction

Goal: show careful handling of evidence and uncertainty.

Navigate & clickOpen Incident Reconstruction → choose the validated reconstruction scenario → scroll only enough to reveal the recorded / inferred distinction and missing-evidence area.
Speak

“This view reconstructs an incident timeline while separating what is recorded from what is inferred. It also flags missing evidence instead of fabricating a connection. That distinction is important for a defensible investigation workflow.”

11

Screen 5 — FIR Registration & Evidence Intake

Goal: show this is a workflow application, not only analytics.

Navigate & clickOpen FIR Registration & Evidence Intake → show the form and the evidence upload area → upload one prepared harmless image, PDF or short CCTV sample only if the page is ready.
Speak

“Drishti supports structured FIR intake and traceable evidence metadata. The prototype records the file details and checksum, creating a clear link between the evidence item and the case workflow.”

Do not fill every form field live. Show the workflow and one successful result.
12

Screen 6 — Case Progress

Goal: show supervisory value.

Navigate & clickOpen Case Progress → click Analyse lifecycle → point to the review queue and station bottlenecks.
Speak

“This is the supervisory view. It highlights lifecycle bottlenecks and cases that may need review. It supports attention to process and workload; it does not judge people or determine enforcement action.”

13

Screen 7 — Patrol Plan

Goal: finish with an actionable district-level output.

Navigate & clickOpen Patrol Plan → select Bangalore Urban → keep Balanced plan → click Plan deployment → point to the “Bangalore Urban only” map label.
Speak

“The patrol plan is constrained to the selected Bangalore Urban boundary. Coordinates outside the district are excluded rather than being treated as patrol zones. This gives a supervisor a clearer, district-ready starting point for planning.”

14

Screen 8 — AI Evidence & Confidence

Goal: close with responsible AI and deployment proof.

Navigate & clickOpen AI Evidence & Confidence → briefly show the guardrails / confidence material. If the data-source status is visible, show it once.
Speak

“Finally, Drishti keeps AI assistance bounded by confidence, evidence and human review. The live prototype is deployed on Zoho Catalyst: AppSail hosts the application service and Catalyst Data Store provides the connected relational development data.”

Closing line — say this exactly

“Drishti turns disconnected FIR intelligence into an explainable, reviewable workspace for investigation and command — built on Zoho Catalyst. It helps officers reach context faster while keeping validation and accountability with the authorised human decision-maker.”

ShowOne strong end-to-end story.
