DESIGN INTENT

One traceable view of an investigation.

Drishti is designed around the officer’s workflow—not around isolated models. Each recommendation links back to FIR records, supporting identifiers and explicit caveats for supervisor review.

Find relationships

Search FIRs, people, phones, vehicles and narrative clues from a single starting point.

Review evidence and progress

Bring evidence intake, incident reconstruction, lifecycle bottlenecks and quality checks into the same case context.

Support accountable action

Surface explainable leads and district-scoped planning signals; officers remain responsible for validation and decisions.

PRODUCTION TARGET

Designed to scale without moving trust into a black box.

A production deployment separates transaction processing, analytical workloads and model operations. This keeps the officer-facing workflow responsive while preserving traceability, privacy and operational control.

Production operating principles

Operational systems remain the source of truth. Analytics and ML are isolated, versioned and measurable; they generate review priorities—not autonomous enforcement decisions.

01 · PERFORMANCECache read-heavy district aggregates; run costly graph and forecast refreshes asynchronously.
02 · DATA CONTROLStore media separately from metadata; enforce retention, encryption and access policy by case role.
03 · MLOPSTrain from approved feature sets, publish versioned endpoints, monitor drift and retain evaluation evidence.
04 · ACCOUNTABILITYLog source records, confidence, human overrides and final action for every material recommendation.
ARCHITECTURE RATIONALE

Why this design is appropriate for police intelligence.

Fast workspaces, controlled data

AppSail gives the team a single governed service boundary. The user interface calls APIs, while Data Store access remains server-side and policy-controlled.

Relational truth before derived intelligence

FIR, person, vehicle, arrest and chargesheet relationships remain structured and queryable. Derived links must always point back to those source records.

Analytics that do not block operations

Heavy computations are moved to scheduled or event-driven jobs in production. The case workspace reads prepared features and aggregates rather than re-training models on every request.

ML as decision support

Production models should use approved features, documented evaluation and versioned endpoints. Confidence must be visible; outputs are never treated as proof or automatic action.
